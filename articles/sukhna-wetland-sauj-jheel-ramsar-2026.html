Sukhna Wetland and Sauj Jheel join the Ramsar List

A 15 October announcement adds two very different wetlands: an urban-edge reservoir and a Ganga floodplain lake.

Reviewed on 17 October 2026. PIB confirms the designations, locations and announced total of 103. Site numbers, ecological details and proposed management actions remain unverified; see caveats below. Items marked "background" are general knowledge, not taken from the sources.

Key facts

  • The Convention on Wetlands announced Sukhna Wetland and Sauj Jheel as new Ramsar sites on 15 October. Its release and the Environment Ministry put India’s announced total at 103.
  • Sukhna Wetland (site 2602) lies beside Chandigarh at the foot of the Outer Himalayas. It is a peri-urban reservoir whose catchment affects water and sediment flows.
  • Sauj Jheel (site 2603) is a shallow lake in Uttar Pradesh’s Ganga floodplain, near Saman Bird Sanctuary. The Convention describes it as a habitat for waterbirds and reports more than 30,000 birds there regularly.
  • The Convention on Wetlands was adopted at Ramsar, Iran, in 1971 and entered into force in 1975. It entered into force for India on 1 February 1982.

Why the two sites matter

The settings are different. Sukhna is next to a city, and its catchment affects water and sediment flows; runoff and erosion are reasonable management questions to examine, though they are an inference from those facts. The Sauj site note lists seasonal fishing limits, restrictions on fine-mesh nets and control of cattle access among proposed management actions. In an answer, connect the site to its catchment and local use rather than treating the Ramsar label as the whole story.

Prelims lines

Sukhna is in Chandigarh; Sauj Jheel is in Uttar Pradesh. The Convention was adopted in 1971 at Ramsar, Iran, and came into force in 1975. India joined in 1982.

Mains angle

Wetland conservation depends on what happens around the water body too: catchment erosion, invasive species, fishing practice and grazing can all shape the result. The Ramsar site notes give two local examples to make that point concrete.
