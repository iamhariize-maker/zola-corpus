Cabinet approves NSAP continuation; release sets three-month guideline window

The Cabinet approved continuation through 2030–31 and higher disability assistance. Its release says revised guidelines will be issued and implemented within three months; current operational status was not independently checked.

Checked against the sources below on 17 October 2026. Items marked "background" are general knowledge, not taken from the sources.

Key facts

  • The Union Cabinet approved ₹53,428 crore to continue the National Social Assistance Programme from 2026–27 to 2030–31. The release says the approval covers 3.20 crore beneficiaries across all 36 States and Union Territories.
  • The programme is to be called Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojana. The approved component names include Pradhan Mantri Vridhavastha Pension Yojana (old age), Pradhan Mantri Vidhawa Pension Yojana (widow), Pradhan Mantri Divyangjan Pension Yojana (disability) and Pradhan Mantri Parivar Labh Yojana (family benefit).
  • For disability pension, the approved assistance is ₹375 for ages 0–79 and ₹625 from age 80. The benchmark-disability threshold changes from 80% to 40%, and the minimum-age rule changes to cover people from birth.
  • The Cabinet release says guidelines setting the revised eligibility, coverage and assistance are to be issued and implemented within three months.
  • Article 41 of the Constitution is the public-assistance directive. It asks the State to act within its economic capacity and development.

What has and has not changed

The Cabinet has approved these changes. Its release says guidelines setting the revised disability-pension eligibility, coverage and assistance are to be issued and implemented within three months. Their current publication status has not been independently checked here, so keep approval and implementation separate.

The broader social-assistance link is Article 41 of the Directive Principles. The Department of Rural Development describes NSAP as a centrally sponsored programme, with States and Union Territories identifying beneficiaries. That older programme note predates the current renaming and pension approval.

Prelims lines

Know the 2026–27 to 2030–31 continuation period and the ₹53,428-crore outlay. The disability-pension changes are separate from the old-age, widow and family-benefit schemes, which continue in their existing form. Article 41 is in Part IV of the Constitution.

Mains angle

The announcement can support an answer on social protection: adequacy of assistance, coverage of persons with disabilities and the gap between Cabinet approval and delivery rules. Article 41 itself is framed within the State’s economic capacity and development, so resource and implementation questions belong in the discussion too.
