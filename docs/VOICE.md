# Voice for Zola study articles

Owner's rule (16 October 2026): the articles should read the way a serious aspirant writes notes for the exam.
Plain, direct, a bit informal, no filler. Not the voice of a press release, a brochure or a chatbot.

## Do
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence where you can.
- Say the fact, then say why it matters for the exam. "Halzen got the physics prize for IceCube. For Prelims, know the
  name and the detector. Mains angle: big science projects and who pays for them."
- Use "you" and "we" when it helps. "Keep this in mind", "remember the difference", "this is a trap".
- Keep the Indian English words an aspirant uses: lakh crore, Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, Mains, prelims, GS-I.
- Write the answer-note angle: definition, example, one line on the way out.
- Say what you do not know. "The report does not say which university. Check before you write it."

## Do not
- No AI tells: "delve", "landscape", "tapestry", "crucial", "pivotal", "navigate", "in today's rapidly evolving world",
  "it is important to note", "in conclusion", "a testament to", "multifaceted", "robust framework".
- No long dash runs. Use full stops. At most one dash in a paragraph.
- No triple-bullet lists of adjectives. No bold for every term.
- No predicting questions ("this is likely to come", "expect this in the exam"). The app never does that.
- No hype: no "must read", "most important", "guaranteed".

## Shape of one article
1. **Headline**: what happened, in one line a student would say out loud.
2. **Quick facts**: name, date, number, place, source. Only what a source states.
3. **Why it matters**: 2 to 4 sentences in the owner's voice.
4. **Prelims lines**: 3 to 5 facts to remember, each one line.
5. **Mains angle**: one question an examiner could ask, framed as a question, with 3 points to cover. This is
   a way to think, not a prediction.
6. **Check this**: what is unverified, what sources disagree on.
7. **Sources**: links with the publisher and date.

## Example (before and after)

Before (AI-sounding): "The Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2026 represents a pivotal milestone in our understanding of
molecular asymmetry, underscoring the multifaceted interplay between chance and selection."

After (exam-note voice): "Chemistry Nobel 2026 went to Kagan and Soai. Their work shows how a tiny imbalance can get
amplified in a reaction. Why it matters: life uses only one hand of many molecules, and nobody is sure why. For
Prelims, remember 'autocatalysis' and that chirality means mirror-image molecules. Mains angle: is the origin of life
a chemistry question or a luck question? Check: the official citation is on nobelprize.org, not re-read here."
